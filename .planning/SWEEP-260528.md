## 2) Simulation logic — how it actually works today

### 2.1 The actual flow

```
SEEDING ── orchestrator (qwen3.6:27b, ~30s load)
  └─ extract entities + sentiment from rumor          [1 call]
  └─ generate 10 bracket modifiers                    [1 call]
  └─ unload orchestrator (~5s)

ROUND 1 ── worker (qwen3:8b, ~30s load)
  └─ for each of 100 agents: see {persona_prompt + market_context + rumor}
       NO peer context.                               [100 calls, NUM_PARALLEL=4]
  └─ ~35-45 min on M1 Max

ROUND 2
  └─ for each of 100 agents: read top-10 R1 posts ranked by
       `coalesce(INFLUENCED_BY.weight, author.influence_weight_base)`
       sorted DESC. R1 has no INFLUENCED_BY edges, so this collapses
       to "top 10 by static influence_weight_base."
  └─ for each agent: see {persona + rumor + peer_context}
       NO market context (R1-only).                   [100 calls]
  └─ compute INFLUENCED_BY edges from cited_agents
  └─ ~35-45 min

ROUND 3
  └─ same as R2 but reads R2 posts. INFLUENCED_BY edges from R2 now
       exist, so ranking starts to reflect real citation patterns.
  └─ ~35-45 min

POST-R3
  └─ narrative generation: 100 more worker calls (~12-25 min)
  └─ unload worker, load orchestrator (~30s)
  └─ advisory synthesis: 1 orchestrator call (~2-5 min)
  └─ TOTAL: ~2-3 hours per cycle
```

### 2.2 What's actually load-bearing vs ceremonial

| Step | Adds signal? | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity extraction | Yes | 1 call | Tickers route to market provider. Necessary. |
| Modifier generation | Marginal | 1 call | Adds ~12-word entity-aware suffix per bracket. With qwen3:8b worker, this doesn't change decisions much. **Possible drop candidate**. |
| Round 1 | **Yes — the prior** | 100 calls | Captures the "uninfluenced" reading. The single most valuable round. |
| Round 2 | Mixed | 100 calls | Conservative bias bloc dominates peer context (see §3). Many agents flip toward consensus. |
| Round 3 | Limited | 100 calls | At this point, most flips have already happened. Adds <10% additional signal in practice. **Strongest cut candidate.** |
| Narrative generation | UI nice-to-have | 100 calls | Only used for per-agent interview tooltips and advisory rationale. **Could be lazy on-demand.** |
| Advisory synthesis | Yes | 1 call | The actual deliverable. Necessary. |

### 2.3 Convergence behavior

The 3-round cascade was designed to model Stanford Generative Agents-style
opinion propagation. In practice on this stack:

- **Round 1 → Round 2: high flip rate.** Each agent sees 10 posts from
  authority brackets (Sovereigns/Whales/Suits/Insiders). Smaller models
  (qwen3:8b) follow context heavily. Expect 30-50% of agents to flip toward
  the highest-confidence peer signal.
- **Round 2 → Round 3: low flip rate.** Citations from R2 mostly point to the
  same authority bracket peers. The system has already converged toward what
  those peers thought.
- **Net effect:** R3 mostly amplifies R2's bias rather than producing new
  information.

### 2.4 Recommendations on round count

| Option | What it gives you | What you lose |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep 3 rounds, fix peer-context bias** (see §3) | Genuine "see what peers think → reconsider" dynamic; more dispersed final distribution. | None except complexity. |
| **B. Cut to 2 rounds** | ~33% wall-clock reduction (~25-30 min saved per cycle); ~20% fewer GPU tokens. Most of the convergence value is in R2 anyway. | Lose the "second look" — R3 occasionally surfaces dissent that R2 missed. |
| **C. Single round, multi-pass scoring** | Run R1 with 100 agents once, then have the orchestrator do a single "swarm summary + dissent extraction" pass over the R1 corpus. ~75% wall-clock reduction. | Lose true peer influence dynamics; becomes "polling + summarization" rather than "cascade." Closer to what the advisory step already does. |

**Recommendation: B (cut to 2 rounds) is the highest-value change.** Wall-time
goes from ~2-3 hours → ~80-100 min. You preserve the round-over-round opinion
shift dynamic that drives interesting flip metrics. If R2 quality drops
noticeably after the cut, the diagnosis is peer-context bias (fix §3) not
round count.

## 3) Swarm composition — is the population representative?

### 3.1 Current composition (100 agents)

### 3.2 Comparison to actual market structure

Real-world equity market dollar volume is **dominated by intermediaries**
(market makers, HFT) and **institutional flow** (Vanguard, BlackRock, pension
funds, hedge funds). The current swarm collapses all of that into "Suits" at
10% and over-represents:

- **Sovereigns at 10%.** Real sovereign wealth + central bank equity activity
  is ~3-5% of daily flow. Their *strategic* weight is huge but their *tactical*
  voice on a single rumor is small.
- **Insiders at 10%.** Insider trading (legal disclosed or not) is rare. 2-3%
  would be more realistic.
- **Policy Wonks at 5%.** Almost no one trades on policy commentary as their
  primary signal. 2% is plenty.

…and **under-represents**:

- **Suits (traditional institutional).** Real market: 40-60% of dollar volume.
  Current swarm: 10%.
- **Quants/Agents (systematic).** Real market: 60-80% of trading volume by
  share (HFT-inclusive). Current swarm: 25%.
- **Macro funds.** Real market: 10-15% of hedge fund AUM. Current swarm: 10%
  (probably fine).

### 3.3 Structural bias built into peer-context ranking

`read_ranked_posts` sorts peers by `coalesce(infl.weight,
author.influence_weight_base) DESC`. In Round 2 (no INFLUENCED_BY edges yet),
this collapses to a static ranking dominated by Sovereigns/Whales/Suits.

**Every one of the 100 agents in R2 sees the same top-10 peers.** A Degen
agent gets the same Sovereigns+Whales context as a Quant. There's no
information diversity — only authority concentration.

Combined with qwen3:8b's tendency to follow heavily-weighted context, this
*manufactures* convergence toward the conservative-bloc median signal.

## 4) Orchestrator path — what it does and what it costs

### 4.1 Roles

The orchestrator (`qwen3.6:27b-q4_K_M`, ~17.4 GB) loads four times per cycle:

1. **Seed injection** (`seed.py`): entity extraction (~30s load + 1 call + 5s unload).
2. **Modifier generation** (`config.py:generate_modifiers`): 1 call, same session as #1.
3. **Advisory synthesis** (`advisory/engine.py:synthesize`): post-R3, ~30s load + 1 call + 5s unload.
4. *(Optional)* HTML report — separate code path not currently wired into the
   web flow.

### 4.2 What's working

- Single advisory call ranks all holdings, applies confidence×exposure
  reranking, and returns a 1-3 paragraph `portfolio_outlook` + per-holding items.
- Bounded retry on ValidationError (one shot, then raise).
- Auto-trigger from `SimulationManager.on_complete` works (verified in
  `web/app.py:69` → `simulation_manager.py:207-211`).

### 4.3 Issues

- **Modifier generation may be ceremony** (see §3.4).
- **Advisory prompt sends ~32 holdings × full JSON enrichment for top 15 + sector tag for rest.** Token waste; see B-7.
- **Orchestrator model swap happens twice per cycle.** Worker (~5.9 GB) + Orchestrator (~17.4 GB) > 22 GB; can't keep both warm. Each swap is ~30s of dead time. This is inherent to the M1 Max constraint, not a bug.

### 4.4 Recommendations

| Change | Effort | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Drop modifier generation pending A/B test. | XS | **CHANGE** (see §3.4). |
| Render "other holdings" client-side instead of in the advisory prompt. | S | **FIX** (B-7). |
| **Consider replacing per-cycle advisory synthesis with a streaming "running advisory" updated after each round.** Each round, ask the orchestrator a small question (e.g., "given R2 results, what's the headline shift?"). Total orchestrator time stays similar but the user sees progressive insight instead of a 30-min black box at the end. | L | **CHANGE — exploratory.** Worth a discussion before committing. |

## 6) One-page summary

**The simulation runs end-to-end.** No critical bugs. The slow parts
(~2-3 hours per cycle) are mostly hardware-bound on M1 Max with qwen3:8b
worker and 2.5k-token prompts.

**The biggest non-obvious issue is structural bias in peer context.** Round 2
shows every agent the same authority-bloc top-10, manufacturing convergence
that masquerades as emergent consensus. The dead-code `select_diverse_peers`
function already implements the fix; it just needs to be wired in.

**Round 3 adds limited new signal.** Most flips happen in R2. Cutting to 2
rounds saves ~33% wall-clock with small quality loss — worth A/B testing.

**Swarm composition skews toward authority brackets** (Sovereigns, Whales,
Suits, Insiders combined = 35% of agents and the only ones that show up in
peer context). Real market is mostly Suits + algos. A rebalance toward those
two would be more representative.

**Test debt is small but visible.** 24 of 28 failures are stale assertions
against renamed APIs; one PR clears them.

**Advisory pipeline works** but wastes tokens emitting boilerplate HOLD items
for low-relevance holdings. Easy to fix client-side.
